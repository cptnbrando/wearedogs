# SEN. JOHN CORNYN — ON MARIJUANA SCHEDULING AND HEMP-DERIVED THC

Received July 2026, in response to constituent contact about the Texas THC shelf pull and the November 12th federal hemp deadline. Reproduced in full, unedited.

We are grateful for the reply. It contains real arguments with real citations behind them, which is more than most offices send, and it is the reason we must continue discussion. **We support anybody and everybody who keeps it real and continues the discussion**. My response follows underneath it.

## THE LETTER

> Thank you for contacting me regarding federal laws on marijuana. I appreciate having the benefit of your comments on this important matter.
>
> Since 1996, over 20 states have legalized marijuana in various forms. I am concerned about evidence showing that marijuana can harm the health of those who use it. I support robust research into its medical risks and potential benefits as the Department of Justice works through the regulatory process related to its scheduling under the Controlled Substances Act. Studies show that adults who frequently use marijuana are 3 to 5 times more likely to develop psychosis, schizophrenia, depression, and anxiety. Adolescents aged 13 to 18 who have used cannabis are more than twice as likely to experience mild or moderate depression and over three times as likely to develop major depressive disorder compared to those who have never used marijuana. Frequent marijuana use is also correlated with increased odds of suicide attempts and major depressive episodes. While marijuana is often perceived as less harmful than other drugs and has the potential to treat certain health conditions, 30% of cannabis users in the United States have developed cannabis use disorder, and approximately 1 in 10 people who use marijuana become addicted. I sympathize with those who turn to marijuana for health benefits, but the risks of addiction, cognitive impairment, and mental health issues cannot be ignored.
>
> The 2018 Farm Bill (P.L. 115-334) included a provision that excluded hemp from the definition of marijuana, which is currently a Schedule I controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act. This change in the definition of hemp to an agricultural commodity inadvertently created a loophole that allowed for the creation and sale of unregulated and intoxicating THC products. THC is the psychoactive compound in cannabis that causes a "high." The legal definition of hemp restricted the concentration of delta-9 THC, the most prominent mind-altering agent in marijuana, but left room to manufacture and sell other psychoactive forms of THC that can be derived from non-intoxicating compounds found in marijuana. I voted in favor of the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act of 2026 (P.L. 119-37), which was signed into law on November 12, 2025, and closed the loophole. This legislation included a provision that readdressed the definition of hemp to restrict the commercial production, sale, and distribution of hemp-derived cannabinoid products, which are widely considered to pose health and public safety risks.
>
> I am also concerned about reports that Chinese organized criminal groups are drivers of the illicit marijuana market in the United States. The Department of Justice has prosecuted members of these groups who have smuggled illegal immigrants into the United States and exploited them to work at marijuana grow houses. Reporting also shows that the groups use banned pesticides that can cause significant harm to laborers, law enforcement officers, surrounding residents, and the environment. This is a very serious health and national security threat that I am watching closely as Chairman of the Senate Caucus on International Narcotics Control.
>
> I am honored to represent Texas in the United States Senate. Thank you for taking the time to contact me.
>
> Sincerely,
> John Cornyn

## OUR REPLY

Published in full on the campaign page as [A Letter From One Driver](/fundraiser/texas-marijuana-driver.md). The short version, point by point:

**On psychosis.** The 3-to-5x figures come from studies of daily high-potency use, not from a 5mg seltzer, and causation is still contested. Schizophrenia incidence in Ontario was stable across legalization, and Colorado dispensary density showed no significant association with psychosis emergency visits [\[JAMA Network Open\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2829840). If potency is the risk, the policy that follows is a potency cap and a printed dose — the exact thing Texas just took off the shelf.

**On adolescents.** Past-year use among 12-to-17-year-olds is 8.7%, down from 14.2% in 2011, before any state legalized [\[SAMHSA NSDUH\]](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm), and high school use fell after legalization in 19 of the 21 states with before-and-after data [\[MPP\]](https://www.mpp.org/issues/legalization/adult-use-legalization-corresponds-with-drop-in-teen-marijuana-use/). A licensed store checks an ID. A dealer does not.

**On dependence.** The Senator is close to right, and we will not pretend otherwise: about 20.6 million Americans meet criteria for cannabis use disorder against 27.9 million for alcohol use disorder, and as a share of users the cannabis rate is higher [\[SAMHSA NSDUH\]](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm). What is not comparable is the outcome — 178,307 American deaths a year from excessive alcohol [\[CDC\]](https://www.cdc.gov/alcohol/facts-stats/index.html), 13,701 of them Texans [\[CDC ARDI\]](https://nccd.cdc.gov/DPH_ARDI/default/default.aspx), against no recorded death from cannabis toxicity per the DEA's own fact sheet [\[DEA\]](https://www.dea.gov/factsheets/marijuana). Dependence is a treatment problem. A state jail felony is not a treatment.

**On the Farm Bill loophole.** Agreed that it was one. A drafting problem deserves a better draft: milligram caps per serving and per container, 21-and-over, seed-to-sale testing, child-resistant packaging, a revocable license, and a tax — which is what Gov. Abbott asked for when he vetoed SB 3 [\[Gov. Abbott\]](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-vetoes-senate-bill-3-89r). Prohibition is not a stricter form of regulation; it is the absence of it.

**On Chinese organized crime and banned pesticides.** This is the part we agree with most, and it is the strongest argument against the ban. Compared to licensed product, 94% of illicit samples carried pesticides — averaging 3.4 compounds each — with some unregulated samples running up to 100 times the lead, and mycotoxins in 12% of illicit product versus none of the licensed [\[study coverage\]](https://stratcann.com/news/high-levels-of-pesticides-found-in-illegal-cannabis-vapes/). Those grow houses do not compete with the licensed shop. They inherit its customers the day it closes.

**What we are asking for.** Protect Delta-9 through and beyond November 12th, fund the research the Senator called for while there is still a legal market to research, and give the 9,100 Texas businesses that followed every rule a rule they can survive under.
