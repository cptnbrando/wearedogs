Look at what the July ban has already set in motion. This was a $5.5 billion dollars / yr Texas industry built since 2018, across gas stations, smoke shops, and liquor stores. Over 9,100 licensed hemp retailers and producers woke up on August 1st holding hundreds of thousands of dollars of suddenly unsellable, fully compliant inventory. Storefronts lost 10% to 20% or more of their revenue overnight. The layoffs did not wait for November. Neither did the closures.

And demand did not go anywhere. It never does. The customers who bought lab-tested, taxed, regulated products in July are being handed to the illegal street market in August. The cheap unregulated vapes and cartridges that send people to the ER are the growth industry in Texas now. Every month between now and November that market compounds, untaxed and untested, while New Mexico, Oklahoma, and Arkansas collect the registers Texas emptied.

The case has not changed, only the tense. 13,701 people die from alcohol every year in Texas alone. Alcohol-related deaths in Texas significantly outpace all drug overdoses combined (which peaked around ~5,500 deaths per year in Texas, with fentanyl accounting for ~1,900). 26.8% of Texas adults report heavy drinking. Deaths related to cannabinoids are extremely rare. For the kid at their first party, for the veteran seeking pain relief — would you prefer they had a 5mg Delta-9 seltzer, or liquor?

Delta-9 beverages are the last legal, safest-in-class option left on Texas shelves. They are precisely dosed, lab-tested, age-gated, and taxed. Wiping them out on November 12th does not protect a single Texan; it hands the final piece of a multi-billion dollar market to the street dealer and the state line, for nothing in return.
