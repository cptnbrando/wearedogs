🚗 A LETTER FROM ONE DRIVER — IN REPLY TO SENATOR JOHN CORNYN

Senator, thank you for writing back. Most offices don't. I read it twice, and I want to answer it plainly, as the person your vote actually lands on. I am not an industry, a lobby, or an activist. I drive for Uber in Dallas–Fort Worth. Six hours a day, in my own car, on my own gas. On August 1st the law quietly changed what my job is, and nobody asked me first.

I cannot say that THC is not dangerous. But it does not disappear because you outlaw it. It will exist forever outside Texas, two hours away from certain cities, and every dollar of it is now somebody else's tax revenue. What a ban changes is not whether it exists. What it changes is how much of my paycheck the state gets to take back. The rich do not get drug tested — they get lawyers. They do get arrested sometimes. Justin Timberlake got a DUI in Sag Harbor two years ago in June 2024. But he's rich, and had private counsel by morning and pleaded down to a traffic violation. I would have a retainer I cannot pay.

I pick up dozens of people every week, from all over DFW. I have smelled it on my passengers more than a few times. And here is the new damage come November: if I get pulled over, for whatever reason, now I face felonies. If I look too tired, if I don't speak with the right decorum, it's their word in legally binding testimony over mine. If the officer brought a dog and the dog is barking. I am just doing my job, not breaking the law, and I am the one punished by it.

And if I drank a legal seltzer back on the 4th of July, in California, a month ago, and I am handed the only test the state actually has — a urine or blood screen that reads metabolites, not impairment — I am looking at a DWI. First offense in Texas is a Class B misdemeanor: up to 180 days in county jail and a $2,000 fine [\[Tex. Penal Code §49.04\]](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PE/htm/PE.49.htm#49.04), plus a license suspension of 90 days to a year [\[Tex. Transp. Code §521.344\]](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TN/htm/TN.521.htm#521.344). The license is not a privilege to me. The license is the entire job. And if that same officer finds a sealed gummy in the door pocket, or someone's forgotten vape in the backseat, then my life gets taken by the police: THC concentrate is Penalty Group 2, and any amount under a gram is a state jail felony — 180 days to 2 years and up to $10,000 [\[Tex. HSC §481.116\]](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/HS/htm/HS.481.htm#481.116).

Even if I pass every test clean, their observations, their recordings, their reports — their word alone — means I need a lawyer. And these Uber rides pay me about $6 each. Fifteen dollars an hour some days, twenty on a good one, six hours a day. I have a chip in my windshield from giant acorns that fell on it, and I pray every day I don't get fined for not having the money to fix it. A lawyer in this city wants a retainer, thousands up front, and the court wants fees on top of that for more paperwork and more time in front of the judge.

And I am just an Uber driver. I am just trying to drive people and get back home alive.

It's just that if I was driving with three 30-racks of Coors Light, three bottles of Hennessy, and ten shooters of apple jack Fireball sealed in the trunk, the officer would say "oh, having a little party tonight? Okay, you be safe." Maybe a speeding ticket. Then he drives away. He does not take my life out from under me through litigation and a permanent record.

Meanwhile, federally compliant hemp — under 0.3% delta-9 by dry weight — is explicitly allowed by TSA in a carry-on and in checked baggage [\[TSA\]](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/items/medical-marijuana). The quantity limits make sellable amounts impossible. But there are 50-year-old grandmothers who prefer a 10mg gummy to painkillers, and college kids who forgot one was in the backpack from the flight in. Same sealed package: fine at DFW security, felony on I-35. And the federal permission itself expires November 12th.

The classifications need revision. Legal carry quantities need to at least be discussed. And there has to be a real conversation about being compelled to take a test that cannot measure the thing it is used to prove: the effects last about two hours, the metabolites read positive for up to 30 days. That gap has already cost people their licenses, their fines, their jail time, and their jobs.

Roughly 12,429 Americans were killed in alcohol-impaired driving crashes in 2023, and Texas led every state in the country with 1,699 of them — about 142 a month, more than four Texans every single day [\[NHTSA, 2023 state data\]](https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813726.pdf). With alcohol I can drink as much as I want on the 4th of July, and by the 5th I can blow a breathalyzer and drive away clean. There is no equivalent test for me. There is only an officer's hunch and a lab result from last month.

Federal data shows about 61.6 million Americans used cannabis in the past year — 43.8 million in the past month, and 21.4 million on a daily or near-daily basis, which is now more people than drink alcohol that often [\[SAMHSA NSDUH\]](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm). Half of all U.S. adults say they have tried it at least once [\[Gallup\]](https://news.gallup.com/poll/509399/fully-half-americans-tried-marijuana.aspx). Texas is 31.7 million people in a beautiful country of 341.8 million [\[Census / Texas Tribune\]](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/01/27/texas-population-2025-census/). We just started Y'all Street, and in the same summer we told a $5.5 billion Texas industry to empty its shelves by Friday.

Do y'all listen to any music? Y'all are going to kick Willie Nelson out of Texas!



On psychosis, schizophrenia, depression, and anxiety: The 3-to-5x figures come from studies of daily use of high-potency product. This is valid, and truth be told, today's July 31st ban did target some powerful, high-potency products. 

However, many THC users are not daily users. And low-dose 5mg or 10mg seltzers, have no science that definitively links any of these mental disorders to THC. The direction of causation is still openly contested. At the population level, schizophrenia incidence in Ontario was stable across legalization, and in Colorado dispensary density showed no significant association with psychosis emergency visits [\[JAMA Network Open\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2829840). If potency is the risk — and I think you are right that it is a risk — then the policy that follows is a potency cap and a printed dose. Texas just deleted the only shelf in the state that had a printed dose on it.

On adolescents. This is the argument for a regulated market, not against one. Past-year cannabis use among 12-to-17-year-olds is 8.7%, down from 14.2% in 2011, before any state legalized [\[SAMHSA NSDUH\]](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm), and in 19 of the 21 states with before-and-after data, high school use went down after legalization [\[MPP\]](https://www.mpp.org/issues/legalization/adult-use-legalization-corresponds-with-drop-in-teen-marijuana-use/). 

A licensed store checks an ID. A dealer with a phone does not, and as of August 1st he is the only one left open.

Roughly 20.6 million Americans meet criteria for cannabis use disorder against 27.9 million for alcohol use disorder [\[SAMHSA NSDUH\]](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm), and as a share of users the cannabis rate is higher. What is not comparable is the outcome. Excessive alcohol kills about 178,307 Americans a year [\[CDC\]](https://www.cdc.gov/alcohol/facts-stats/index.html), 13,701 of them Texans [\[CDC ARDI\]](https://nccd.cdc.gov/DPH_ARDI/default/default.aspx). Alcohol withdrawal can kill you by itself. Cannabis toxicity has never been recorded as a cause of death by the DEA's own fact sheet [\[DEA\]](https://www.dea.gov/factsheets/marijuana). Dependence is a treatment problem. A state jail felony is not a treatment.

On the Farm Bill loophole. You are right that it was a loophole — nobody in 2018 intended to license a synthetic delta-8 market with no dose cap, no age gate, and no lab standard. But a loophole is a drafting problem, and the answer to a drafting problem is a better draft: a milligram cap per serving and per container, 21-and-over, seed-to-sale testing, child-resistant packaging and warnings, better testing and advertising restrictions, a license that can be pulled, and a tax. That is what Governor Abbott asked for when he vetoed SB 3 in June 2025 [\[Gov. Abbott\]](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-vetoes-senate-bill-3-89r).

Prohibition is not a stricter version of regulation. It is the absence of it.

On Chinese organized crime and banned pesticides. This is the part of your letter I agree with most, and it is the strongest argument against the ban you voted for. When researchers compared illicit product to licensed product, 94% of illicit samples carried pesticides — averaging 3.4 different compounds each — against trace levels in two licensed samples, with some unregulated samples running up to 100 times the lead and mycotoxins in 12% of illicit product and none of the licensed [\[study coverage\]](https://stratcann.com/news/high-levels-of-pesticides-found-in-illegal-cannabis-vapes/). Those illegal grow houses you are watching do not compete with the smoke shop on Jefferson. They inherit its customers. Every store Texas closed on August 1st and will close in November is a store that took that market share away from them, tested the product, carded the buyer, and paid tax on it. We just handed a $5.5 billion market back to the exact operators you named — and every customer in it, roughly 5.7 million Texans. 18% of Texas. 1 in 5 Texans.

Senator, I am not asking you to tell Texas that marijuana is harmless. It isn't. Cannabis-related emergency visits were 896,418 in 2023 and rising [\[SAMHSA DAWN\]](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/pep23-07-03-001.pdf), and I would rather argue from that number than hide it. I am asking for the same things this country already decided alcohol should have: a legal age, a printed dose, a lab behind the label, a license that can be revoked, a tax that funds the roads I drive on — and a test that measures whether I am impaired right now instead of whether I existed in July.

Protect Delta-9 through and beyond November 12th. Fund the research you called for while there is still a legal market to research. And give the 9,100 Texas businesses that spent seven years following every rule you wrote a chance to survive.

Until then I'll be out on 635 at two in the morning, driving people home from the bars. From the drug you didn't ban. For six dollars a ride. Please don't make that a felony too.
