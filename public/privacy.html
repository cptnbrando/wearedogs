Privacy policy

Effective 27 October 2026 · covers wearedogs.net and dogs.red (the same site)

Like any website, our hosts and external services receive standard connection information when they serve pages or assets. The like button adds device signals only after you press it. A browser that remembers an earlier like may also request the current count when the info page opens; that request includes your IP for rate limiting but sends no device fingerprint.

What happens when you like DOGS

The note beside the thumbs-up describes this before you click. When you press it, your browser:

  1. Your browser saves two small items in its own storage (localStorage, not cookies): a random ID for this browser (wad-device), and the fact that you liked plus the like count (wad-likes).
  2. Your browser reads a few traits of your device to build a device fingerprint: browser name (without version numbers), operating system platform, languages, time zone, screen size and colour depth, CPU core count, memory and touch support, how your graphics hardware describes itself, and how your device draws a small test image and renders a short silent test sound. Each trait is reduced to a short hash on your device; the raw values never leave it. These client-side hashes are not cryptographic and some low-variation traits may be guessable, so treat them as pseudonymous data.
  3. Cloudflare Turnstile, an invisible check that you are a person rather than a bot, runs in the background. To do that, Cloudflare processes information such as your IP address and browser details under its Turnstile privacy addendum.
  4. The hashes, random ID and Turnstile result are sent to our API (api.wearedogs.net or api.dogs.red), which runs on Cloudflare. The request also carries standard connection data, including your IP address; the Worker uses it for rate limiting and derives a keyed hash for repeat checks.

What we store

  • the random ID, hashed again with a secret key
  • your IP address, hashed with a secret key (for IPv6, only the network half: your connection, not your device)
  • each device-trait hash, hashed again with a secret key
  • the time you liked

We never store your raw IP address or raw device information, and without our secret key the stored hashes cannot be turned back into them. Your like itself is just +1 on the count, a number with nothing attached to it.

Why

To discourage repeat likes without making anyone sign up, and to limit bots inflating the count. A repeated random browser ID is blocked; otherwise a like may be treated as a repeat when it comes from the same network as a recent like and enough device signals match. This is an abuse deterrent, not a guarantee that one person can only like once. We do not use these signals to follow you across other sites, build a profile, show ads, or sell or share them. Cloudflare runs the service on our behalf.

Legal basis

Your consent, given by clicking the like button after reading the disclosure beside it and this policy. If you never click it, the device fingerprint and Turnstile check do not run. A returning browser may still make the count-refresh request described above.

How long we keep it

Your like is forever: the count only ever goes up. The fingerprint record behind it (the hashes and the time) is scheduled for deletion at the first hourly cleanup after it becomes 20 days old. Cloudflare outages or exhausted account D1 limits can delay that cleanup. The like control reopens at the cleanup boundary, so you can like DOGS again.

Your browser keeps treating you as having liked until the first hourly cleanup after 20 days. It keeps its two stored items until you clear them.

Your choices and rights

  • Don't click like, and nothing is collected.
  • Clear this site's data in your browser settings to remove the two stored items.
  • Because we only keep keyed hashes, we cannot tell which stored record is yours, so we cannot look it up, export it or delete it individually before the cleanup after the 20-day mark. You can still ask us anything about it, or object, using the email us card on the info page.
  • You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Hosting

Like any website, the hosts that serve these pages see standard request information such as IP addresses: GitHub Pages for wearedogs.net, and Cloudflare for dogs.red, our media and our API.

We load fonts from Google Fonts; Google may receive standard connection information when your browser requests those files. Some pages also load images, audio or video from data.wearedogs.net, served by Cloudflare.
