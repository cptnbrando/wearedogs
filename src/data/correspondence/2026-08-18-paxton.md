# SEN. ANGELA PAXTON — ON SB 3, THE DSHS RULE TIMELINE, AND THE FEDERAL HEMP BAN

Received August 18th 2026, in response to constituent contact about the Texas THC shelf pull and the federal hemp deadline. Reproduced in full, unedited.

**This is the most helpful message we have received so far, and our favorite.** Senator Paxton co-authored SB 3 — she is on the opposite side of this issue from us — and her office still sent the single clearest account anyone has given us of how Texas got here: the SB 3 veto, the executive order, the May 1st Texas Supreme Court decision reinstating the 2021 DSHS rule, the July 10th Texas Register notice and the July 31st effective date with the statute that sets the clock, the Senate's interim charge and the July 7th hearing on it, and the federal ban with the correct jurisdictional handoff to Senators Cornyn and Cruz. She noted our position for next session and explained her reasoning instead of dodging it. That is exactly what we ask for. **We support anybody and everybody who keeps it real and continues the discussion.**

One note on the dates: the letter cites the federal ban's original November 12th 2026 effective date. The Senate's August stopgap has since pushed that deadline to December 11th 2026.

## THE LETTER

> Fellow Texan,
>
> Thank you for contacting my office regarding your opposition to removing access to consumable hemp products in Texas. I appreciate you taking the time to share your thoughts with me. As your State Senator, I am committed to listening, learning, and leading on the issues most important to you, and I have made note of your position on this topic.
>
> For background, consumable hemp products (CHPs), including hemp-derived cannabidiol (CBD), are legal in Texas only if they contain no more than 0.3% delta-9 tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). The proliferation of CHPs in Texas was a topic of significant discussion during the most recent regular and special legislative sessions. Specifically, this was due to concerns regarding children accessing intoxicating products containing THC, including products that exceeded the legal THC limit.
>
> To protect Texas children, during the 89th Regular Legislative Session, I co-authored Senate Bill 3. This bill would have prohibited the sale of consumable hemp products containing any amount of THC while expressly allowing CBD to remain legal, and would have subjected CHPs to strict regulation. Although the Texas Legislature passed this legislation, Governor Abbott vetoed Senate Bill 3. When alternative legislation failed to pass during the subsequent special sessions, Governor Abbott issued an executive order that prohibited the sale of CHPs to minors and directed the Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS) to strengthen their rules and improve enforcement of the rules and laws governing CHPs in Texas. However, ongoing litigation has impacted the enforceability of the new DSHS rules.
>
> On May 1, 2026, the Texas Supreme Court reversed an injunction that had blocked the enforceability of a 2021 DSHS rule that clarified that CHPs containing tetrahydrocannabinols other than delta-9 THC (such as delta-8 THC) were illegal in Texas. With the injunction no longer in effect, DSHS published a notice in the Texas Register on July 10, 2026, that the reinstated definitions would take effect on July 31, 2026. This timeline is pursuant to Sec. 2001.036, Texas Government Code, which specifies that a rule posted in the Texas Register can take effect 20 days after the date on which it is filed in the office of the Secretary of State.
>
> The Texas Legislature is not currently in session so while the Senate cannot pass any legislation at this time; we utilize the legislative interim, the time between legislative sessions, to research and evaluate solutions to key issues. This preparation is essential as we look ahead to the next legislative session, which begins in January 2027. The Lieutenant Governor charged the Senate Committee on Health & Human Services with the following interim charge relating to the consumable hemp industry:
>
> Addressing Societal Impacts of THC Product Consumption: Study the impact of THC on increased health care costs, mental health emergency detentions, and the risk of being diagnosed with a THC-induced psychotic disorder. Make recommendations to decrease health care and criminal justice costs.
>
> The committee convened on July 7, 2026, to hold a hearing on this interim charge. You may access a recording of this hearing on the Texas Senate website here.
>
> As you noted in your correspondence, the federal government has passed updated restrictions on CHPs. In addition to restrictions related to total THC content, this new law also prohibits products containing synthetic or unnatural CBD. This ban goes into effect November 12, 2026.
>
> As a state legislator, I do not have jurisdiction on federal legislation progressing through the United States Congress, including any measures that would postpone or overturn the federal consumable hemp product restrictions that are currently scheduled to go into effect in November.
>
> Your U.S. Senators, U.S. Senator John Cornyn and U.S. Senator Ted Cruz, make decisions on legislation before Congress and have jurisdiction over federal agencies, such as the Food & Drug Administration (FDA), if you would like to share your thoughts with them. Their contact information is listed below:
>
> U.S. Senator John Cornyn - https://www.cornyn.senate.gov/
>
> U.S. Senator Ted Cruz - https://www.cruz.senate.gov/
>
> Thank you again for contacting me and engaging in the legislative process. Please be assured that I will take your correspondence into consideration as legislation relating to consumable hemp products is discussed in the Texas Senate next session. Our representative democracy is strengthened by your participation, and I desire to listen to my constituents, learn from their experiences and perspectives, and lead in the Great State of Texas.
>
> Blessings,
>
> Angela Paxton<br>
> Texas State Senator, District 8

## MY REPLY

I actually didn't realize CBD products were also banned as of December 11th 2026. I have a lot of questions and will send a proper reply soon. How did the 0.3% THC limit get decided? What was the evidence presented that showed delta-8, delta-10, THCP, and THCA products impacted the mental health of consumers worse than delta-9 products?

I can't lie about the dangers of THC. I can imagine a teenager who has never consumed cannabis, drinking a 25mg seltzer at a party, saying to his friends "I don't feel nothing! I'm gonna drive home y'all." And then a half hour into his drive, it is quite a sudden, powerful, dangerous thing especially without experience. And Texas roads are not easy to drive. But, California roads are much trickier, and marijuana is entirely legal there with a valid 18 year old driver's license. That product is much stronger than anything here in the midwest.

Was the senate shown any data regarding California's adoption, and other legalized states? In Oklahoma here you need a medical card, but it ends up being just an entirely unverified market where you text a ghost kitchen guy, send him $200, then give his signature to OMMA, send them another $200 and you're good to visit any of the 1,500 shops here. There's nothing medical about it that the OMMA provides, they just soak up significant taxation, and prevents these businesses from using modern systems like credit cards.

I've been seeing medical marijuana stores come up around Texas too, with prescriptions and doctors, I have never visited one. Frankly I don't enjoy government tracking my smoking habits, especially if it can bite me in the ass years later with increases in insurance rates. They don't track the alcohol stores they don't track the bars they don't track the cigarettes, but I'm sure the insurance companies want to. I really think Texas, and America would be better off if we just treated marijuana the same as alcohol, with some caveats. Certainly not imprisoning people for 10 years for possession, or growing out of their garages. More information, restriction of certain stronger products until you're a returning customer.

These products are $5.5 billion dollars in annual revenue, and they are in every state in America. They aren't going anywhere, no matter how many people go to jail or how many millions go to the government in fines. The reality is that the teenager who has never used cannabis is, and has been driving on Texas state and federal roads since 2015 when these seltzers were popularized, or since the 90s when Dr. Dre was around. Texas should help inform the public to help that teenager, because the product will always exist.
