# SEN. TED CRUZ — ON STATE REGULATION OF HEMP AND THE COMMITTEES THAT HOLD THE PEN

Received August 21st 2026, in response to constituent contact about the federal hemp ban and the December 11th deadline. Reproduced in full, unedited.

This one matters. Texas' junior Senator — in writing — says a blanket federal prohibition of hemp-derived products "disenfranchises the voters in each of the fifty states," that regulation should rest with each individual state, and that a one-size-fits-all federal standard "will create unintended consequences that harm consumers." That is closer to our position than any federal reply we have received, and we thank him for engaging. **We support anybody and everybody who keeps it real and continues the discussion.** He also names the two Senate committees that hold jurisdiction over hemp legislation, notes he sits on neither, and commits to keeping our input in mind if a related vote comes before him. So we looked up the committees he pointed to — who is on them, and how to reach them. That information follows the letter.

## THE LETTER

> Thank you for contacting me regarding the regulation of hemp-derived products in Congress. Input from fellow Texans significantly informs my decision-making and allows me to better represent our state.
>
> I have long believed that the regulation of hemp and marijuana products should rest with each individual state. Reasonable minds can disagree, and a blanket federal prohibition of hemp-derived products disenfranchises the voters in each of the fifty states. A one-size-fits-all federal standard will create unintended consequences that harm consumers. There is a vital need to protect children by, at a minimum, requiring that purchasers be 21 and prohibiting synthetics and dangerous foreign imports marketed to kids.
>
> Legislation affecting hemp falls under the jurisdiction of the Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions and the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry. Although I am not a member of either Committee, rest assured I will keep your input in mind should related legislation come before me for a vote.
>
> Thank you again for sharing your views with me. Please feel free to contact me in the future about any issue important to you or your family. It is an honor to serve you and the people of Texas.
>
> For Liberty,
>
> Ted Cruz<br>
> United States Senator

## THE COMMITTEES HE NAMES

Senator Cruz points to two committees as the ones holding jurisdiction over hemp legislation. Here is who they are and how to reach them.

### Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions (HELP)

- **Size:** 23 senators — 12 Republicans, 11 Democrats/Independents.
- **Chair:** Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA). **Ranking Member:** Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT).
- **Texans on the committee:** none.
- **Contact:** 428 Dirksen Senate Office Building, Washington, DC 20510 · (202) 224-5375 · [help.senate.gov](https://www.help.senate.gov/)
- **Membership roster:** [help.senate.gov/about/members](https://www.help.senate.gov/about/members)

### Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry

- **Size:** 23 senators — 12 Republicans, 11 Democrats.
- **Chair:** Sen. John Boozman (R-AR). **Ranking Member:** Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN).
- **Texans on the committee:** none.
- **Contact:** 328A Russell Senate Office Building, Washington, DC 20510 · (202) 224-2035 · [agriculture.senate.gov](https://www.agriculture.senate.gov/) · [contact form](https://www.agriculture.senate.gov/contact)
- **Membership roster:** [agriculture.senate.gov/about/membership](https://www.agriculture.senate.gov/about/membership)

Two committees, 23 seats each, and not a single Texan in either room — while Texas carries the largest hemp market in the country. Committee mail gets read: letters to the chair and ranking member at the addresses above, and to individual members through their own offices (every senator's contact form is at [senate.gov/senators](https://www.senate.gov/senators/)), are how a bill's language gets shaped before it ever reaches Senator Cruz's desk for the floor vote he describes.
