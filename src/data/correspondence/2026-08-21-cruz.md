# SEN. TED CRUZ — ON STATE REGULATION OF HEMP AND THE COMMITTEES THAT HOLD THE PEN

Received August 21st 2026, in response to constituent contact about the federal hemp ban and the December 11th deadline. Reproduced in full, unedited.

This one matters. Texas' junior Senator — in writing — says a blanket federal prohibition of hemp-derived products "disenfranchises the voters in each of the fifty states," that regulation should rest with each individual state, and that a one-size-fits-all federal standard "will create unintended consequences that harm consumers." That is closer to our position than any federal reply we have received, and we thank him for engaging. **We support anybody and everybody who keeps it real and continues the discussion.** He also names the two Senate committees that hold jurisdiction over hemp legislation, notes he sits on neither, and commits to keeping our input in mind if a related vote comes before him. So we took the hint: all 43 senators on those two committees are now on the campaign map and wired into the mail room. The buttons follow the letter.

## THE LETTER

> Thank you for contacting me regarding the regulation of hemp-derived products in Congress. Input from fellow Texans significantly informs my decision-making and allows me to better represent our state.
>
> I have long believed that the regulation of hemp and marijuana products should rest with each individual state. Reasonable minds can disagree, and a blanket federal prohibition of hemp-derived products disenfranchises the voters in each of the fifty states. A one-size-fits-all federal standard will create unintended consequences that harm consumers. There is a vital need to protect children by, at a minimum, requiring that purchasers be 21 and prohibiting synthetics and dangerous foreign imports marketed to kids.
>
> Legislation affecting hemp falls under the jurisdiction of the Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions and the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry. Although I am not a member of either Committee, rest assured I will keep your input in mind should related legislation come before me for a vote.
>
> Thank you again for sharing your views with me. Please feel free to contact me in the future about any issue important to you or your family. It is an honor to serve you and the people of Texas.
>
> For Liberty,
>
> Ted Cruz<br>
> United States Senator

## THE COMMITTEES HE NAMES

Senator Cruz points to two committees as the ones holding jurisdiction over hemp legislation: the Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor & Pensions (23 senators, chaired by Sen. Bill Cassidy of Louisiana) and the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition & Forestry (23 senators, chaired by Sen. John Boozman of Arkansas). Three senators sit on both, so that's **43 senators holding the pen — and not one of them is a Texan**, while Texas carries the largest hemp market in the country.

So we put all 43 of them where the rest of the roster lives: on the campaign map, anchored offshore in violet, wired into the same mail room as everybody else. Committee mail gets read — it's how a bill's language gets shaped before it ever reaches Senator Cruz's desk for the floor vote he describes.
