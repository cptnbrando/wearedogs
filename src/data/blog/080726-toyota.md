Frank's father was the Cloudflare engineer who pioneered the lava lamp wall. An ingenious creation of the United States in 2017, the system relied on a bank of 100 motion-captured lava lamps in San Francisco in order to inject unguessable quantum entropy into Cloudflare’s global cryptography network. Because of this system, almost 70% of every login system on planet earth websites apps cars etc had a reliable source of a random number.

Randomness is almost non-existent in our normal natural world. Get a big sheet of graph paper, and take 100 grains of rice. Blindfold yourself, and throw them up. Where they land, on the graph paper, take their distance from 0,0, their location values. Now, are those grains of rice in random locations, random coordinates, random numbers across the graph paper?

Well, since computers don't like rice, when you ask a computer a machine to generate a random number from 1 to 1,000, it will usually begin by asking the clock system what the current date and time is, usually in UTC (Coordinated Universal Time), that is time since January 1, 1970 (the unix epoch). For instance, on August 18th, 2046 at 2:47pm in Beijing, if you ask any computer in the world for the current UTC, unless something's fucked up, it will say 2,418,187,620 seconds, aka 76 years, 7 months, and 17.28 days elapsed since the Unix epoch began on January 1, 1970.

Now, time is not random, but since UTC and time is always moving forward, every single moment every second the computer is asked for a random number, that UTC seed helps make a random number appear since it is different from nanosecond to nanosecond and will never repeat. Time isn't the only piece of the puzzle, but it increases the entropy of the output, the predictablity. Still, especially using quantum computing, if you know that current UTC seed and know the right algorithms, you can sometimes guess what the random number output is going to be. And if you guess the random number output, when you sign in to Facebook, and your computer sends a random number to Facebook servers which uses that number as a decryptable hash to verify that your password is valid and your session is real and authentic, well, you know this shit is easier in theory than it is in practice.

Because of Frank's dad, the clock wasn't the only thing that can give you a reliably different value for any given second of any given time. Back in late 2015, Frank's dad took 100 lava lamps, plugged them in, attached 4k UHD cameras to their bases, and recorded their "lava", the oil in the water, the shadows, the blobs moving up and together and splitting and bouncing. The cameras fed into software that translated the video feeds into raw data. And that was how Frank got random image color location size random data. Random 1s and 0s that are unpredictable, and should rarely repeat (100 lamps with oil all in the same location as they were before). More entropy for random algorithms, more keys to the random puzzle, and better security and trust in the things we don't think about. Facebook sign ins, text messages, emails, online shopping financial transaction, streaming subscriptions, credit card swipes, prescription information, school district portals, flight check-ins, and essentially almost every tap on every phone screen in the world.
