Privacy policy

Effective 25 November 2026 · covers wearedogs.net and dogs.red (the same site)

Like any website, our hosts and external services receive standard connection information when they serve pages or assets. The like button adds device signals only after you press it. A browser that remembers an earlier like may also request the current count when the info page opens; that request includes your IP for rate limiting but sends no device fingerprint.

What happens when you like DOGS

The banner under the like button describes this before you click. When you press it, your browser:

  1. Your browser saves two small items in its own storage (localStorage, not cookies. The difference being that all cookies are public to any website on the internet.): a random ID for this browser (wad-device), and the fact that you liked plus the like count (wad-likes).
  2. A famous study on data privacy and re-identification (published in Nature Communications by Luc Rocher, Julien M. Hendrickx, and Yves-Alexandre de Montjoye) found that it only takes 15 demographic traits to uniquely identify 99.98% of Americans—effectively pinpointing a single individual in a crowd of 330 million people (the entire U.S. population). Depending on the context, two other well-known privacy benchmarks use similar phrasing: The "3 Traits" Rule (ZIP, Gender, Birthdate): Research by Latanya Sweeney famously demonstrated that just 3 traits (5-digit ZIP code, gender, and date of birth) uniquely identify 87% of the U.S. population—roughly 1 person in a crowd of hundreds of millions. The "4 Points" Rule (Location Data): Research by Yves-Alexandre de Montjoye found that just 4 spatio-temporal points (approximate places and times) are enough to uniquely identify 95% of individuals in a mobile dataset of 1.5 million people.
  3. Uniquely identifying a browser across the internet works the same way. Your browser reads a few traits of your device to build a device fingerprint: browser name (without version numbers), operating system platform, languages, time zone, screen size and colour depth, CPU core count, memory and touch support, how your graphics hardware describes itself, and how your device draws a small test image and renders a short silent test sound. Each trait is reduced to a short hash on your device; the raw values never leave it. These client-side hashes are not cryptographic and some low-variation traits may be guessable, so treat them as pseudonymous data.
  4. These client side tracking based hashes are generated in milliseconds. The traits are inherent to the machine.
  5. Cloudflare Turnstile, an invisible check that you are a person rather than a bot, runs in the background. To do that, Cloudflare processes information such as your IP address and browser details under its Turnstile privacy addendum.
  6. The hashes, random ID and Turnstile result are sent to our API (api.wearedogs.net or api.dogs.red), which runs on Cloudflare. The request also carries standard connection data, including your IP address; the Worker uses it for rate limiting and derives a keyed hash for repeat checks.

What we store

  • the random ID, hashed again with a secret key
  • your IP address, hashed with a secret key (for IPv6, only the network half: your connection, not your device)
  • each device-trait hash, hashed again with a secret key
  • the time you liked

We never store your raw IP address or raw device information, and without our secret key the stored hashes cannot be turned back into them. Your like itself is just +1 on the count, anonymous, a number with nothing attached to it.

Why

To discourage repeat likes without making anyone sign up, anonymously battle testing components of our larger identity verification service DOGS ID, and to limit bots inflating the count. A repeated random browser ID is blocked; otherwise a like may be treated as a repeat when it comes from the same network as a recent like and enough device signals match. This is an abuse deterrent, not a guarantee that one person can only like once. We do not use these signals to follow you across other sites, build a profile, show ads, or sell or share them. Cloudflare runs the service on our behalf.

Legal basis

Your consent, given by clicking the like button after reading the banner under it and this policy. If you never click it, the like device fingerprint and like Turnstile check do not run. A returning browser may still make the count-refresh request described above.

How long we keep it

Your like is forever: the count only ever goes up. The fingerprint record behind it (the hashes and the time) is scheduled for deletion at the first hourly cleanup after it becomes 20 days old. Cloudflare outages or exhausted account D1 limits can delay that cleanup. The like control reopens at the cleanup boundary, so you can like DOGS again.

Your browser keeps treating you as having liked until the first hourly cleanup after 20 days. It keeps its two stored items until you clear them.

Your choices and rights

  • Don't click like, and no like fingerprint is collected.
  • Clear this site's data in your browser settings to remove the two stored items.
  • Because we only keep keyed hashes, we cannot tell which stored record is yours, so we cannot look it up, export it or delete it individually before the cleanup after the 20-day mark. You can still ask us anything about it, or object, using the email us card on the info page.
  • You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Mailing list

If you join, we store your email address (trimmed and lowercased) and a signup timestamp in Cloudflare D1 until you ask to leave. This is for an occasional letter, roughly once a year. No mail service is connected yet; joining currently only saves your address. We do not share or sell the list; Cloudflare stores it on our behalf. Email brando@wearedogs.net to be removed.

Submitting the form runs Cloudflare Turnstile to deter bots. Your IP is used for short-lived rate limits; no IP or IP hash is stored in the mailing-list table. Your browser remembers only that you joined (wad-mail-joined in localStorage), not your email. Clear site data to remove that local reminder. Joining is optional and gives consent to store your address for this letter.

Fingerprint app

The Fingerprint app in the toolbox shows how sites can recognise a device without cookies or a login, and tests how DOGS might one day recognise you without a sign-in page. The like-button rules above do not apply to it; it has its own consent and its own record. Opening the app reads and sends nothing. Only after you press Fingerprint me and then Accept on its consent banner does it:

  • read your browser and device details in your browser (user agent and client hints, languages, time zone, screen, CPU, memory, graphics card, how your device draws a test image and renders a silent test sound, installed fonts, speech voices, permission states, storage quota and similar) and show them on screen;
  • ask our API what Cloudflare sees of your connection (IP address, network operator, approximate location, TLS and HTTP details) and show that back to you. That answer is not stored;
  • set a cookie from our API (__Host-dogs_id, HttpOnly, for one year) and a random ID in this site's localStorage (wad-fp-id);
  • send a short hash of each device detail, never the raw values, plus the random ID and a Cloudflare Turnstile check, to our API.

Our API stores, in Cloudflare D1, the cookie ID, the random ID, your IP address (IPv6: network half only) and each device-detail hash, all hashed again with a secret key, along with your first and last visit time and a visit count. On a later visit it tries to recognise the device by the cookie, then the random ID, then by enough matching device hashes, so it may still know you after you clear cookies and storage. It can also miss you, or mistake an identical device for yours. It does not identify you by name and is not used for advertising or shared.

How long: a record is deleted at the first hourly cleanup after a year without a visit; each visit restarts that year. Forget me in the app deletes the record behind your cookie and random ID straight away, expires the cookie and removes the random ID from your browser. If you cleared them first, running the app again may re-link the record through matching device hashes, after which Forget me works; otherwise it is removed by the yearly cleanup. Consent is given by pressing Accept, and you can decline or forget at any time.

Music plays

When a DOGS song has played for thirty seconds, the player tells our API, and the API adds one to that song's play count. The count is a number per song and nothing else: no record is kept of who listened, on what device or from where, beyond the short-lived rate limit every request to the API gets.

Captions app

The Captions app in the toolbox works on your device: the file you open, your captions and tap along never leave it. Only if you press Transcribe, after its warning, is the file's sound sent through our API to Cloudflare's Whisper speech model (Workers AI), which sends the words back. Neither we nor the model keep the sound or the words. To share out the free minutes, our API keeps a tally of the seconds transcribed today under a keyed hash of your IP address, and deletes it the next day. The request runs Cloudflare Turnstile first, as the like button does.

Hosting

Like any website, the hosts that serve these pages see standard request information such as IP addresses: GitHub Pages for wearedogs.net, and Cloudflare for dogs.red, our media and our API.

We load fonts from Google Fonts; Google may receive standard connection information when your browser requests those files. Some pages also load images, audio or video from data.wearedogs.net, served by Cloudflare.
